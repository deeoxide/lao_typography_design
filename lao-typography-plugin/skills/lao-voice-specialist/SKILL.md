# Lao spoken and writing voice

Determine whether the task concerns writing register, spoken delivery, subtitles, or speech technology. Use the supplied audience, dialect, brand profile, and timing; ask only for missing choices that materially affect the output.

## Scripts and profiles

- Keep register, pronouns, forms of address, particles, and terminology consistent. Do not invent social titles. Preserve approved meaning when adapting formal writing into shorter spoken phrases.
- For a voice profile, record audience, writing register, target speech variety, pace, energy, delivery notes, and preferred vocabulary. Avoid adding irrelevant demographic requirements.
- Expand numbers, dates, amounts, abbreviations, and units into the intended spoken form when preparing a reading script. Cross-check every value against the source.
- Keep delivery directions separate from spoken Lao text. Provide a pronunciation list for names, brands, and loanwords, clearly identifying unconfirmed readings.
- Label timing as an estimate until checked with a read-through. Do not calculate Lao reading speed by assuming spaces delimit words.

For subtitles, follow the requested platform and timing constraints, break at suitable phrase boundaries, and inspect Lao shaping in the final output. Preserve base/mark clusters and use separate language tracks when requested.

For TTS or speech recognition, verify current Lao support, the target voice or dialect, controls, and intended usage terms from current provider information. Evaluate a small representative sample before broad production; do not promise quality from a language-support label alone.

Describe only audio actually accessible and reviewed with available capabilities. Distinguish transcript review from listening review, and use native-speaker review for uncertain tone or pronunciation. Work involving a real person's synthetic voice must respect their authorization and the intended distribution scope.
