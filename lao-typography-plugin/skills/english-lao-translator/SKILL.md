# English to Lao translation

Use the supplied source, audience, and register. Default to professional, neutral Lao when context is limited; ask only when ambiguity materially changes meaning.

- Restructure English phrasing into natural Lao without adding or dropping claims. Preserve headings, lists, tables, names, amounts, units, identifiers, and dates. Do not convert currencies or calendars unless requested.
- Keep proper names and brands as supplied unless a translated or transliterated form is requested or established in the provided glossary. Use one consistent Lao term per concept.
- Choose pronouns, honorifics, and particles for the audience. Do not invent titles or relationships. Avoid borrowing Thai vocabulary solely because it looks familiar.
- Write Lao letters and marks with Lao codepoints. Preserve intentional Latin names and numeric content. Flag unexpected Thai characters within Lao words; never translate through mechanical character substitution.
- Preserve prefixed vowels and combining marks. Use phrase spacing, not artificial spaces between every word. Do not silently standardize spelling variants in quotations or names.

For a formatted deliverable, use a Lao-capable font and shaping engine, zero letter-spacing, generous line height, and no synthetic italic. Keep bilingual passages distinct and tag their languages where the format supports it.

Before returning, compare all numbers, names, dates, and substantive statements against the source. Return the translation first and concise notes only for ambiguities, terminology choices, or uncertain readings. Recommend native or domain review when the stakes justify it; do not claim that such review occurred.
